# CEO review

Mode: **scope reduction** for the business; **hold scope** for the prototype.

## Nuclear challenge

The holding-company framing is premature. It assumes great recipes, transferable rights, production feasibility, retail pull, and shared operations all at once. The company-killing failure is manufacturing several emotionally compelling products before any earns a reorder. Current state → family seller guessing at costs → selected wedge: paid feasibility screen → next proof: one compliant local pilot → future state: a portfolio only after repeatable reorders.

## Eleven-section review

1. **Premise/wedge:** Charge for killing bad launches early. A “business in a box” is too broad; one recipe, one city, one channel is testable.
2. **ICP/JTBD:** Target an existing micro-seller with money and evidence, not every grandmother with a recipe.
3. **GTM:** Shared kitchens, market managers, and grocers concentrate qualified supply and truth. Creator partnerships come after reorder, not before.
4. **Economics:** Include landed freight, channel margin, spoilage, returns, promotions, testing, insurance, and working capital before production approval. The demo exposes only a first screen and says so.
5. **Moat:** Brand design and directory access are commodities. Verified process fit plus reorder data can compound.
6. **Architecture:** Deterministic calculation beats simulated AI. Version every assumption; treat manufacturer claims as expiring data.
7. **Errors/data paths:** Name blank input, nonpositive values, impossible pricing, low margin, short shelf life, budget gap, and no match. Input changes invalidate stale output.
8. **Security:** Recipe theft or family-story misuse destroys trust. Split access, log exports, and capture ownership/approval terms before intake.
9. **Observability:** The core dashboard is cost-estimate error, screen conversion, time to verified match, sell-through, returns, and reorder—not signups.
10. **Failure modes:** Foodborne illness, allergens, unsupported shelf life, ownership disputes, false vendor claims, negative landed contribution, dead inventory, and family conflict each need a named gate and owner.
11. **Tests/UX:** Preserve validation, loading, blocked, passed, selection, no-match, channel recalculation, and mobile overflow. Keep every fictional claim visibly labeled.

## Decisions applied

- **Accept:** one deterministic first-run screen, local-retail-first wedge, explicit cash/margin/shelf-life gates, shared fictional fixtures, honest static deployment boundary.
- **Improve:** clear results on input changes, name every blocker, make manufacturer selection a real second interaction, and lead with the family story without sentimentalizing the risk.
- **Defer:** formulation AI, real manufacturer integrations, packaging generation, retailer portal, inventory, payments, revenue share, multi-brand operations, and paid acquisition.
- **Reject:** launching a portfolio before reorder proof; implying the screen certifies food safety or vendor capability.

## Failure modes that kill trust

Publishing a formulation without owner approval, representing a stale certification as current, green-lighting an unsafe process, hiding negative landed contribution, or trapping a family in ambiguous rights. Any one is a company-level incident.
