# Office Hours: Heirloom Launch

Generated 2026-09-11 · Startup diagnostic · Unattended, evidence-limited

## Position

“A holding company for Grandma’s recipes” starts with too much capital, too many SKUs, and no proof of repeatable demand. The narrow paid wedge is a **$750 family-recipe launch screen for one shelf-stable product**: determine whether the story, process, margin, first-run cash, and manufacturing constraints justify a four-week local-retail pilot.

## Six forcing questions

### 1. Demand reality

The source shows enabling infrastructure and small-brand category growth, not demand for this operator. No recipe owner, retailer, or buyer has committed money. Evidence that changes the verdict: three families pay for the screen, one local retailer issues a written trial order, and one product reorders after sell-through.

### 2. Painful status quo

An entrepreneurial family member has a beloved recipe, a kitchen-scale cost guess, and scattered advice from designers, shared kitchens, and online manufacturer directories. They do not know whether the process is commercially safe, a co-packer will accept the batch, packaging economics support wholesale, or $10k–$25k disappears before demand is tested.

### 3. Desperate specificity

Initial customer: the adult child or grandchild, age 30–55, already selling 50–200 units monthly at farmers markets or community events. They are the family’s operator, can invest $10k+, and need to answer, “Can this become a compliant 1,000-unit run with 55%+ gross margin and one credible retail test?” Their failure is burning family money—and trust—on inventory nobody reorders.

### 4. Narrowest paid wedge

One recipe, one city, one channel. Deliver a process-risk checklist, bottom-up unit economics, verified co-packer outreach shortlist, label/compliance dependency map, and 100-unit pre-sell plan. The prototype demonstrates the deterministic screening step. It does not create brands, certify safety, or contract manufacturers.

### 5. Observation and validation

No real user was observed. Watch five existing market sellers cost a batch and call a co-packer without help. Record where yield, ingredients, shelf life, MOQ, and wholesale margin estimates fail. Then pre-sell one founder story to two independent grocers before commissioning packaging.

### 6. Future-fit

Manufacturing search and creative production will keep getting cheaper. That makes generic launch services less essential. The business wins only if it compounds trusted retailer sell-through data, process/manufacturer fit, and a reputation for preserving the family’s voice while killing weak economics early.

## Premises

1. The recipe is not scarce; a repeatable path from kitchen batch to reorder is scarce.
2. Local-retail proof is a cheaper truth source than national DTC acquisition.
3. Family trust requires explicit recipe ownership, approval rights, and exit terms before brand work.
4. A screen must be valuable even when its answer is “do not launch.”

## Approaches considered

- **A — Paid launch screen (minimal, recommended):** S effort, medium risk. One deterministic brief plus human vendor verification; fastest way to charge for truth, but services-heavy.
- **B — Four-week local launch sprint:** M effort, medium-high risk. Adds packaging, 100-unit pilot, retailer outreach, and sell-through measurement; stronger evidence, more execution exposure.
- **C — Multi-brand holding company:** XL effort, high risk. Shared operations and portfolio economics could compound, but only after several repeatable launches.

## Assignment

Recruit five family-recipe sellers already moving at least 50 units per month. Charge three of them $750 for the same screen, secure one written retailer trial, and refuse to build packaging until gross margin and ownership gates pass.
