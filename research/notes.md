## Useful source summary

The opportunity is a shared launch operator for family food recipes: the recipe owner contributes the product story and formulation, while an umbrella company handles branding, positioning, creator partnerships, manufacturing discovery, and go-to-market in exchange for a revenue share. The source points to improved manufacturing discovery—Keychain reports a searchable network of 28,000 food manufacturers, while PartnerSlate positions co-packer matching as a days-not-months workflow—and cites the broader claim that small independent brands represented 15% of US food-and-beverage sales but drove 35% of category growth by 2025. It also uses high-protein CPG operator Dropout/Jams as directional precedent.

Those signals do not prove that families will license recipes, that unit economics work, or that retailers want a portfolio. No public scores, keyword volume, offer ladder, pricing, difficulty score, revenue estimate, or detailed GTM notes were accessible, so none are invented. All product economics in this repository are explicit fictional assumptions.

## Product interpretation

The prototype narrows the holding-company thesis to the paid decision immediately before expensive packaging and production: a first-run feasibility screen. The operator tests margin, launch cash, shelf life, channel drag, and co-packer fit, then takes a qualified recipe into a human concierge sprint. The family story is central, but ownership, recipe rights, food safety, and vendor claims remain human-verification gates.
